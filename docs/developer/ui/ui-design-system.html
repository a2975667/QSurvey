Design System V3 – Colors & Buttons

Material-3-style roles and status colors using your palette, plus elevated buttons. Negative and critical colors are separated: gold for common negatives, terracotta for rare critical cases.

Principles & Preferences

This app should feel muted, legible, and consistent. Color is used sparingly for structure and status, not decoration.

  • Muted palette, few colors. Core brand colors are light blue #A6C2CE, olive #A6C29B, and gold #EBC57C, plus a small set of greys (#F7F7FA, #FFFFFF, #DDDDDD, #6E799C, #666666).
  • No bright blue. The old bright blue #27AAFE is removed from the design language; do not reintroduce it.
  • Semantic roles first. Use brand blue-grey as primary interaction (#6E799C), light blue as tonal/container (#A6C2CE), olive for constructive/positive actions, and gold for warning/“negative but non-critical” states. Terracotta #C86547 / soft #FBE8E2 is reserved for truly critical actions.
  • Text is mid-grey, not black. Prefer dark neutral text (#20222A, #1F2430) and mid-grey (#666666) for button labels and helper text, especially on light/tonal buttons.
  • Material 3-ish hierarchy. Primary buttons are filled + slightly elevated; tonal and outline buttons provide secondary emphasis; chips and pills carry status/metadata, not extra color for its own sake.
  • Less copy, more structure. Favor clear headings, sections, and chips over long paragraphs of instructional text. Keep QS/Likert/Text editors organized into labeled sections with settings at the bottom.
When in doubt, start from these roles and reuse existing colors rather than introducing new hex values. See also docs/developer/ui/ui-tokens.md for semantic naming.
